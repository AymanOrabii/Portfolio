Courses & supervision

Teaching

Teaching History and Social Studies through historical inquiry, critical thinking and the interpretation of primary sources, with an emphasis on connecting past events to contemporary societies and global perspectives.

The study of history is not simply about understanding the past; it is about learning to question, interpret and understand the world we live in.

Courses

This academic year
HIS 201 Undergraduate

World History: Ancient Civilisations

An introduction to the political, social, economic and cultural development of ancient civilisations, examining Ancient Egypt, Mesopotamia, Greece and Rome through historical sources and archaeological evidence.

Term
Autumn, lectures Mon & Wed 11:00
Format
22 lectures, 8 labs, one field day
Students
About 120, second year
Weekly outline
  1. Introduction to ancient civilisations and historical evidence
  2. Ancient Egypt: society, government and religion
  3. Mesopotamia: early cities, writing and political systems
  4. Ancient Greece: democracy, philosophy and culture
  5. Ancient Rome: empire, law and society
  6. Archaeological discoveries and the study of material culture
  7. Comparing ancient civilisations and their contributions
  8. 1. Historical source analysis and final review

HIS 331 Undergraduate

The Modern World: Conflict and Transformation

An examination of major political, social and economic transformations from the late nineteenth century to the twentieth century, focusing on imperialism, nationalism, the First World War and the emergence of a new international order.

Term
Spring, plus one week in May
Format
Six workshops and a five-day field week
Students
Up to 32, third year
Weekly outline
  1. The origins of modern imperialism and nationalism
  2. The causes and outbreak of the First World War
  3. Major military campaigns and historical turning points
  4. The impact of war on societies and civilians
  5. The collapse of empires and the new world order
  6. Historical source analysis and research presentations

SOC 512 Graduate

Social Studies: Society, Culture and Global Citizenship

An exploration of human societies, cultural diversity, social institutions and global interdependence. Students examine citizenship, human rights, sustainable development and contemporary social issues through critical discussion, case studies and collaborative learning.

Term
Autumn, Thu 14:00–16:00
Format
Seminar, 10 sessions
Students
6–10 MSc and PhD
Weekly outline
  1. Understanding society, culture and social institutions
  2. Cultural diversity and social identity
  3. Citizenship, democracy and human rights
  4. Globalisation and international interdependence
  5. Sustainable development and contemporary social issues
  6. Case studies, group discussions and presentations

HUM 590 Graduate

Humanities: Culture, History and Human Experience

An interdisciplinary exploration of human civilisation through history, philosophy, literature and cultural studies. Examines the development of ideas, traditions, values and social institutions across different periods and societies, encouraging critical thinking and a deeper understanding of human experience.

Term
Spring, Tue 10:00–12:00
Format
Workshops and proposal review
Students
About 25, first-year PhD
Weekly outline
  1. Introduction to the humanities and human civilisation
  2. The development of ideas, beliefs and philosophies
  3. Ancient civilisations and cultural achievements
  4. Religion, literature and artistic expression
  5. Cultural heritage and historical interpretation
  6. Interdisciplinary research and final presentations
